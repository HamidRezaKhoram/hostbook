# Research Notes

Research done on 2026-11-23 to ground the guide and the product. Each finding lists its sources. Many sources are lifestyle or vendor blogs; the **Confidence** column says how much weight to put on each finding.

## 1. Hosting principles

| Finding | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Start from a specific purpose, not the category ("a dinner"). ## 2. Conversation and group size

| Finding | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Spontaneous conversation caps at about 4 people | High (peer-reviewed, Dunbar et al.) | [Oxford ORA: Dunbar et al.](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:2abf4992-f4d6-4e3f-ae9c-18ee3bbf9c58/files/m66165e705d8a8f52d425c2f9103adead); [Size and structure of freely forming conversational groups](https://www.neuroscience.ox.ac.uk/publications/945657); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jessica-stillman/4-is-magic-number-for-great-conversations-new-research-reveals.html); [Quartz](https://qz.com/work/1388086/why-cant-more-than-four-people-have-a-conversation-at-once) |
| Mean conversation group 2.75; no group > 6 stays one conversation | High | Same Oxford sources |
| Dyads are the most common group in daily life (N = 4,398) | High | [PMC7769262](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7769262) |
| Face-to-face preferred over side-by-side unless > ~5.5 ft apart | Medium (older study) | [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/out-of-the-ooze/201707/what-is-the-right-size-for-a-group-conversation) |

**Implication for the app:** a seating/"conversation clusters" helper is low priority, but the guide should teach small clusters. The guest list could flag "connectors."

## 3. Attendance and RSVPs

| Finding | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Free events: ~28% median no-show; paid ~17%; small free (10–49) ~37% | Medium-High (real check-in data, public events) | [PheedLoop Event Data Lab #05](https://pheedloop.com/blog/event-data-lab-report-5) |
| Vendor estimates for free RSVPs range 30–60% | Low (marketing) | [Ticket Fairy estimator](https://ticketfairy.com/tools/no-show-rate-estimator); [Nunify](https://www.nunify.com/blogs/why-people-rsvp-but-dont-attend-corporate-events); [Eventbrite UK](https://www.eventbrite.co.uk/blog/how-to-manage-attendance-free-events-ds00/) |
| Multiple reminders noticeably improve attendance | Low-Medium (anecdotal) | [Apartment Therapy Partiful review](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/partiful-review-37193743) |

**Gap:** no data on no-shows for private friend parties. Our 10–20% figure is a heuristic. The app should let the host see their own history (yes → came) to replace it over time.

## 8. Existing apps (competitor scan)

| App | Strength | Gap Hostbook fills |
|---|---|---|
| **Partiful** | Beautiful invites, phone-number RSVP, text blasts, auto reminders | No built-in item-claim / bring-list found; character limits on blasts; account-based, data on their servers |
| **SignUpGenius** | Slot-based sign-ups, reminders, potluck templates | Clunky on mobile, ads, a generic "sign-up" tool rather than a party planner; no run sheet or arrival times |
| **SignUp.com** | Similar slots, cleaner UI | Same |
| **Perfect Potluck** | Free, no ads, potluck and meal trains | Single purpose |
| **Google Forms / group chat** | Everyone has it | Nothing structured; duplicates, lost messages |

Sources: [Partiful help](https://help.partiful.com/hc/en-us/articles/26526377667739-Why-use-Partiful); [Apartment Therapy Partiful review](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/partiful-review-37193743); [SignUpGenius compare](https://www.signupgenius.com/resources/compare-sign-up-tools); [Zelos roundup](https://getzelos.com/apps-for-sign-up-sheets); [SignUp.com no-login tools](https://mobileapp.signup.com/compare-free-potluck-and-school-event-planning-tools-that-dont-require-participants-to-create-an-account-or-log-in).

**Hostbook's angle:** a *host's* private cockpit (guests, who brings what, who arrives when, run sheet, notes, calculator, built-in guide) with per-guest share links. No accounts, no ads, it's free, and it runs from GitHub Pages.
